Where curiosity learns its first ten moves.

Cognitive development, focus, and the basic movement of every piece — introduced the way children actually learn: through play, repetition, and small wins.

01

Focus, in short bursts

Attention span is built like a muscle. Sessions are structured in short, rewarding stretches so young players stay locked in without burning out.

02

Notation from day one

Reading and writing moves — e4, Nf3, O-O — becomes second nature early, so the language of chess never feels foreign later.

03

Ten patterns, not theory

Rather than memorised openings, beginners learn the ten tactical shapes — forks, pins, back-rank ideas — that decide most games at this level.

Level 02 · The Strategy Lab

Calculate deeper. Plan sharper.

Middle-game tactical calculation, pattern recognition, and planning — the toolkit that turns a player who knows the rules into one who wins on purpose.

01

Opening repertoires

A small, well-understood set of openings for both colours — built for the club player, not memorised for a database.

02

Middlegame planning

Reading a position's imbalances and choosing a plan before calculating a single line — the skill that separates intermediate from advanced.

03

Endgame technique

Rook endings, opposition, and conversion — the technical foundation that turns a winning position into an actual win.
